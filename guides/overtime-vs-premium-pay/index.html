Overtime vs premium pay: what's the difference?

Overtime is extra pay for working more hours than a threshold; a premium is extra pay for working at a particular time or in a particular category, like a weekend or a night. They are not the same thing, and a single hour can carry both at once — which is why they are worth tracking separately.

Last reviewed: 2026-07-16

Two different ideas

Overtime is about quantity. Once you pass a daily or weekly hours threshold, hours beyond it are paid at a higher rate.

Premium pay is about timing or category. Certain hours — a weekend, a night, a public holiday — are paid at a higher rate regardless of how many hours you have worked.

Because one measures how much you worked and the other measures when, the same hour can qualify for both.

Why they are not automatically the same

It is tempting to treat any higher-than-usual pay as "overtime," but the two are triggered by different things. You can work a short weekend shift that earns a premium without any overtime, or a long weekday shift that earns overtime without any premium. Keeping them distinct is what lets a record show why an hour was paid the way it was.

Stacking: when both apply at once

When an hour is both overtime and a premium, a rule decides how they combine — this is often called stacking. Common approaches are to add the two extras together, to multiply them, or to apply only the higher of the two. Different workplaces choose different approaches, so the same hour can produce different pay under different rules.

Why workplace rules vary

Thresholds, multipliers, which days count as the weekend and which hours count as night are all set by contracts, agreements and local norms that differ from place to place. There is no single universal rule, which is exactly why a tracker should let you describe your own.

Common mistakes

  • Calling every higher rate "overtime" and missing a separate premium entitlement.
  • Assuming a weekend automatically means overtime, or vice versa.
  • Ignoring the stacking rule when an hour qualifies for both.
  • Copying another workplace's thresholds instead of your own.

What Elmtrackr can't determine

Elmtrackr applies the rules you enter; it does not know the rules that apply to you. It can't tell you what your legal overtime threshold is, which premiums you are entitled to, or how they must stack under your contract or local law. Those are decisions for your agreement and your employer, not the app.

Frequently asked questions

Is weekend pay overtime?

Not necessarily. Weekend pay is usually a premium — extra for when you work — and applies whether or not you are also in overtime. You can earn one without the other.

Can an hour be both overtime and a premium?

Yes. Overtime depends on total hours; a premium depends on timing. A weekend hour past your threshold can qualify for both, and a stacking rule decides the total.

What is stacking?

Stacking is how two overlapping extras combine — for example adding them, multiplying them, or taking the higher one. Workplaces choose different approaches.

Why don't the same rules apply everywhere?

Thresholds, multipliers and premium windows come from contracts, agreements and local norms that vary. That is why a tracker should let you configure your own rather than assume a default.
