How to track your work hours

To track your work hours reliably, record the start and end time of every shift, note any unpaid breaks, and fix mistakes as soon as you notice them. What keeps a month accurate is capturing each entry the moment it happens and keeping it all in one place, rather than reconstructing it later from memory.

Last reviewed: 2026-07-16

What a good shift record contains

A work-hours record is simply a log of when you worked. For each shift, a complete entry usually captures:

  • The date of the shift.
  • The start time and the end time.
  • Any unpaid breaks, so paid time is separated from total time on site.
  • The pay category that applied — regular, overtime, weekend, night or holiday — if your pay changes with it.
  • What you worked on, if you track tasks.
  • A short note when something is unusual, so a future you understands the entry.

Step by step

1. Clock in when you start

Mark the start at the moment you begin, not later. A time captured live is the one you can trust.

2. Account for breaks

If a break is unpaid, make sure your record reflects only paid time — for example by clocking out for the break, or by adjusting the shift afterwards.

3. Clock out when you finish

Mark the end the same way. A clear end time is what turns a shift into a countable number of hours.

4. Correct anything you missed

Forgot to clock in, or started late? Add or edit the shift while the details are still fresh, rather than guessing at month's end.

5. Review at the end of the period

Before payday, skim the period's shifts for gaps or obvious errors. A quick review catches the entry you forgot before it becomes a pay question.

Common mistakes

  • Clocking in or out from memory hours later, which quietly rounds away real minutes.
  • Forgetting to start a shift at all, so it never reaches the record.
  • Not separating unpaid breaks from paid time.
  • Keeping times in scattered places — a note here, a message there — that never add up.
  • Leaving corrections until payday, when the details are already fuzzy.

What Elmtrackr can't determine

A tracker records what you tell it; it does not know your contract. Elmtrackr can't decide whether a particular break is paid or unpaid for you, whether your recorded times match your employer's own system, or what you are legally entitled to. Those depend on your agreement and your workplace, and your employer's records remain the official source.

Frequently asked questions

What information should I record for each shift?

At minimum the date, start time and end time. Adding unpaid breaks, the pay category and a short note makes the record far more useful when you check it later.

Do I need to track breaks?

If a break is unpaid, yes — otherwise your hours will look longer than the time you are actually paid for. Record it so paid time is separated from total time on site.

What if I forget to clock in?

Add the shift after the fact and set the correct start and end. Doing it while you still remember the details keeps the record honest.

Where should the record be kept?

In one place you control. Elmtrackr keeps your shifts in a local history on your device, with optional export.

Can I track hours from a watch?

Yes. A Wear OS companion lets you clock in and out from the wrist and syncs back to your phone.
