How to review your work hours against a payslip

To check a payslip, gather your own record of the month's shifts and compare it line by line with the payslip: regular hours first, then each overtime bracket, then premiums, breaks and any reimbursements. Where they differ, note it clearly and raise it with payroll — the goal is a documented question, not a verdict.

Last reviewed: 2026-07-16

What you are comparing

A review puts two records side by side: your own log of when you worked, and your employer's payslip for the same period. You are looking for differences worth asking about — not trying to declare who is right.

Step by step

1. Collect the month's shifts

Pull together every shift in the pay period from your own record, ideally exported so you can tick items off.

2. Compare regular hours

Add up your regular (non-overtime, non-premium) hours and compare the total with the base hours on the payslip.

3. Compare overtime brackets

Check each overtime tier separately — the number of overtime hours and the rate applied — rather than lumping them together.

4. Compare premiums

Match weekend, night or holiday premium hours against the payslip, remembering an hour can be both overtime and a premium.

5. Check unpaid breaks

Confirm that unpaid breaks were deducted the way you expect, and no more.

6. Check reimbursements

If you claimed travel or other reimbursements, confirm they appear.

7. Document any discrepancy

For each difference, write down the date, what you recorded, what the payslip shows, and the gap. A specific note is far easier to resolve than "my pay looks wrong."

8. Raise it with your employer or payroll

Share the documented differences with whoever handles pay. Most are explained quickly; some lead to a correction.

Common mistakes

  • Comparing only the bottom-line total instead of each category.
  • Treating gross estimate and net pay as if they should match.
  • Forgetting that one hour can be both overtime and a premium.
  • Raising a vague concern instead of specific dated differences.
  • Assuming a difference is automatically an error rather than a question.

How Elmtrackr helps

Elmtrackr gives you the independent record to compare with. Monthly and weekly reports break your hours into regular, overtime and weekend totals, reimbursement claims can be gathered into the same report, and you can export a month as CSV or PDF to sit next to the payslip. See work-hours reports.

What Elmtrackr can't determine

Elmtrackr can show a difference; it cannot judge it. It does not decide whether a payslip is legally correct, does not read or import your payslip, and does not know the deductions and agreements behind your pay. It gives you the evidence to ask a good question — your employer's payroll remains the authority on what you are owed.

Frequently asked questions

Does Elmtrackr tell me if my payslip is correct?

No. It gives you an independent record and reports to compare against, but it does not read your payslip or decide whether it is legally correct. Use it to spot and document differences to raise.

Should the estimate match my payslip exactly?

Not usually. An estimate is a gross figure; a payslip reflects taxes, deductions and agreements. Compare categories of hours, and treat differences as questions.

How do I document a discrepancy?

Note the date, what you recorded, what the payslip shows and the size of the gap. Exporting your month as CSV or PDF gives you something concrete to attach.

Who do I raise a discrepancy with?

Whoever handles pay where you work — a manager, HR or a payroll team. A specific, dated difference is the fastest way to get an answer.
